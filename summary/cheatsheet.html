Neobank Stack Cheatsheet

Eleven decisions between a licence and a working bank — the shortlist at each, and what three real banks actually chose. Numbering is a dependency order, not a preference: each layer constrains the ones below it. It starts at 00, directly below — the objective decides the rest.

Part 1
Eleven layers
Aug 2026
00 Design for the user experience first — from the objective Numbered 00 because it happens before layer 01 and decides it. Name the objective, map the use cases and features it demands, then check the stack can deliver them — not the reverse. Every row below is a consequence of this one.
ObjectivePrimacy
Be the main account. Salary in, bills out, card top of wallet
Needs: salary detection, Direct Debit origination, instant balance and notifications, joint accounts, a buffer or overdraft
So the stack must: hold your own sort code for inbound, originate Bacs, and answer a balance read in milliseconds. Primacy makes an outage existential — it buys layers 02, 03 and 11
ObjectiveCheap user growth
Lowest cost to acquire and serve. Sign up in minutes, spend in seconds
Needs: near-instant onboarding, a virtual card at once, free P2P, referral loops, no fee wall on the basics
So the stack must: auto-approve most applicants without a human — manual review is the CAC — and keep cost per dormant account near zero. Buys layers 06 and 04; a sponsor gets you there fastest
ObjectiveProfitability
Earn more per customer than they cost. Interchange, lending, deposits, FX
Needs: credit and overdraft, savings, paid tiers, affordability data, loss control
So the stack must: decision credit and fund from deposits — which needs the licence, not a sponsor — and hold fraud and dispute losses down, because they come straight off margin. Buys layers 01, 07 and 09
The three pull against each other, and that is the real decision. A BIN sponsor is the right answer for growth and a tax on profitability. Your own licence is the reverse. Direct scheme access buys primacy’s reliability and does almost nothing for growth. Chime chose growth and took no charter; Monzo chose primacy and built the rails. Neither stack is better — each is downstream of a different objective. Pick yours before reading a single row below.

Planes, Clocks & Traps

What the eleven-layer grid cannot show: the concerns that cut across layers, the deadlines set outside your company, and the failures that come from treating a plane as a layer.

Cross-cutting

Fraud — touches 10 of 11

most cross-cutting thing in the stack
0102030405060708091011
02Sets the reporting obligation. Under a sponsor, their thresholds become yours by contract — their tolerance decides when you get restricted
03Read latency is a fraud control. Miss the auth deadline and the scheme approves on your behalf, with your checks skipped
04Card fraud: sub-second, no retry. Decided once inside the scheme timeout. No asynchronous fallback
05Scams: in-flight or not at all. The customer authorised it, so nothing looks anomalous. Stop it before the money leaves
06Two jobs, opposite directions. Fraudsters out at onboarding; mules detected later. Owned by a team measured on conversion
07Its own layer — and still not where fraud happens. The platform, models and cases live here; the interventions all execute elsewhere
08AML is a different discipline. Fraud chases customer loss in real time; AML chases regulatory reporting. Shared data, different clocks, different regulators
09First-party fraud lands here. Bust-out looks like credit deterioration until it does not
10One feature store, six consumers. Same velocity, device and behaviour features. Computing them twice is the standard expensive mistake
11The decision log is the defence. Not logged at decision time = does not exist

Chargebacks — touches 5 of 11

nobody owns it end to end by default
0102030405060708091011
02Sets the liability. As principal member the loss is yours. With a BIN sponsor, the sponsor is liable to the scheme then recovers from you by contract
03Two postings, not one. Provisional credit at intake, reversal or confirmation at resolution. Model a contingent balance
04Delivers the message. Thredd, Marqeta and Galileo all expose it by API — enough to launch, rarely enough at volume
08Where the case lives. Evidence, representment, pre-arbitration, hard scheme deadlines
11Closes the loop. A won chargeback is money arriving that must match a settlement file — its own break class

Reversal windows — know before you build

NoneRTP · FedNow. Final on receipt. Credit-push only, so neither can debit a customer
min–hrsFaster Payments. A recall attempt, not a guarantee
2–60dACH. Two banking days for most codes; up to 60 for consumer unauthorised
120d+Cards. Chargeback windows under scheme rules
∞Bacs Direct Debit. Effectively unbounded — indemnity claims under the Guarantee

Best-in-class disputes — build order

  1. Transaction enrichment in the feed — merchant name, logo, location, category. Prevents the query being raised at all
  2. Deadline engine with regulatory and scheme clocks. The one component whose failure is a breach, not a lost case
  3. Deflection at intake — Order Insight / Consumer Clarity. Biggest single lever on cost per case
  4. Contingent-position modelling in the ledger. Provisional credit is real exposure
  5. Auto-decision thresholds by amount, reason code, history. Analyst time is the real constraint
  6. Automatic evidence assembly — device, location, 3DS, session. Table stakes against CE 3.0
  7. Feedback loop into fraud models and merchant scoring
  8. Recovery reconciliation as its own break class

Five traps

Thinking the fraud layer owns fraudIt now has its own layer, and still executes at six others. Six problems — onboarding, ATO, card, scam, mule, first-party — with different deadlines and owners
Buying TM first because it is procurableYou get good case management on a stack too slow to use it. Build card auth and scam interception first
Reading balance latency as a performance metricIt is an authorisation control. Monzo: P99 400–500ms → ~200ms
Booking provisional credit as one postingIt is real money on your book on a case you have not won. Finance must see it from day one
Assuming a licence gets you the railsScheme membership is applied for separately — and "direct" describes the connection, not settlement (DCSP vs DCNSP)
