Fraud — touches 9 of 10

most cross-cutting thing in the stack
010203 040506 07080910
02Sets the reporting obligation. Under a sponsor, their thresholds become yours by contract — their tolerance decides when you get restricted
03Read latency is a fraud control. Miss the auth deadline and the scheme approves on your behalf, with your checks skipped
04Card fraud: sub-second, no retry. Decided once inside the scheme timeout. No asynchronous fallback
05Scams: in-flight or not at all. The customer authorised it, so nothing looks anomalous. Stop it before the money leaves
06Two jobs, opposite directions. Fraudsters out at onboarding; mules detected later. Owned by a team measured on conversion
07Where the platform lives — not where fraud happens. Supplies decisions to four layers, owns the outcome at none
08First-party fraud lands here. Bust-out looks like credit deterioration until it does not
09One feature store, six consumers. Same velocity, device and behaviour features. Computing them twice is the standard expensive mistake
10The decision log is the defence. Not logged at decision time = does not exist

Best-in-class disputes — build order

  1. Transaction enrichment in the feed — merchant name, logo, location, category. Prevents the query being raised at all
  2. Deadline engine with regulatory and scheme clocks. The one component whose failure is a breach, not a lost case
  3. Deflection at intake — Order Insight / Consumer Clarity. Biggest single lever on cost per case
  4. Contingent-position modelling in the ledger. Provisional credit is real exposure
  5. Auto-decision thresholds by amount, reason code, history. Analyst time is the real constraint
  6. Automatic evidence assembly — device, location, 3DS, session. Table stakes against CE 3.0
  7. Feedback loop into fraud models and merchant scoring
  8. Recovery reconciliation as its own break class

Five traps

Treating fraud as a layerIt is six problems — onboarding, ATO, card, scam, mule, first-party — at five layers with different deadlines and owners
Buying TM first because it is procurableYou get good case management on a stack too slow to use it. Build card auth and scam interception first
Reading balance latency as a performance metricIt is an authorisation control. Monzo: P99 400–500ms → ~200ms
Booking provisional credit as one postingIt is real money on your book on a case you have not won. Finance must see it from day one
Assuming a licence gets you the railsScheme membership is applied for separately — and "direct" describes the connection, not settlement (DCSP vs DCNSP)
