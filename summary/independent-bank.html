Building an independent bank

Own licence, own Bank of England settlement account, own scheme membership. No sponsor anywhere. Ten layers, every decision, and the vendors who can actually fill each one.

19 Aug 202610 layers60+ vendors3 worked examples22 sources

This is the hardest configuration and the one with the fewest shortcuts. It is also the one most often described vaguely, so here it is concretely: every layer, the decision it forces, and the real options — including doing it yourself.

One distinction to carry through: owning a layer and building a layer are different things. You can hold card scheme principal membership and still buy the processor. You can be a direct participant settling in your own name and still buy the gateway technology. Independence is about who the regulated entity is — not about writing all the code.

01–02Licence, then two more memberships nobody warns you about

The UK route is two-stage mobilisation: authorisation with restrictions at roughly £1m capital, during which you finish building and testing, then full authorisation at £5m initial capital. Treat mobilisation as an engineering phase with exit criteria — it is the only period where you can rehearse migration and reconciliation with a bounded blast radius.

Then two separate memberships, to two different kinds of institution:

  • Payment schemes — a Bank of England settlement account, RTGS access, and direct participation in Faster Payments, Bacs and CHAPS through Pay.UK. Settlement is prefunded into a segregated, interest-bearing account at the Bank. July 2026's flexible Net Sender Cap model materially reduced that burden — if your business case predates it, redo the numbers.
  • Card schemes — Visa and Mastercard principal membership needs your licence plus collateral, certification, operational infrastructure and ongoing dues into the millions of euros a year. This is the line item founders most often miss entirely.

The nuance that saves a year

Being a direct participant does not mean writing the gateway. You can settle in your own name and still buy the connectivity technology — from Form3, Icon Solutions (IPF), Volante, ACI or Finastra.

You remain the participant and the regulated entity; they supply the pipe. Worth stating explicitly in board papers, because "we use Form3" is routinely misheard as "we are indirect". Monzo built its own gateway — a deliberate Go monolith — but that was a choice, not a requirement of direct participation.

03The ledger, and the decision you cannot unwind

Buy from Thought Machine Vault Core (products as Smart Contracts), 10x SuperCore (polyglot, built-in migration tooling), Temenos Transact, Mambu, Tuum, Finxact or Engine by Starling. Build on TigerBeetle, Formance or Blnk. Or build outright, as Monzo, Starling and Revolut did — months of engineering for the ledger itself, years for reconciliation and operations.

Whichever you pick, the invariants are non-negotiable: sum-to-zero, postings as truth with balances derived, append-only, idempotent writes, bi-temporal records, hierarchical account paths. And the one nobody plans for — checkpoint your derived balances. Monzo hit P99 400–500ms on balance reads before fixing it, and a slow balance read during card authorisation hands your decision to the scheme.

06–07Identity and financial crime

Buy the signals; own the router. Document and liveness from Onfido/Entrust, Jumio, Persona, Veriff or Sumsub; identity data from Experian, LexisNexis or GBG; screening from ComplyAdvantage, Napier or Dow Jones — or self-host OpenSanctions with yente, two Docker containers, so no customer data leaves your infrastructure. KYB entity resolution from Senzing, Quantexa or Splink, the UK Ministry of Justice's open-source linkage engine.

On financial crime: buy to launch — ComplyAdvantage, Unit21, Feedzai, Hawk AI, Featurespace — then build detection once fraud is material. In the UK, APP reimbursement makes it material on day one. The pattern worth copying is Monzo's: controls as sandboxed pure functions, typed into detectors, action controls and action-selection controls, with every execution logged.

The cost of independence, at this layer specifically

As your own principal member you are the issuer of record. Dispute liability is yours directly — there is no sponsor to absorb it, contest it, or argue about root cause with.

So you need the full dispute stack from launch: a platform (Quavo, Pega Smart Dispute, Rivero Amiko), pre-dispute collaboration (Ethoca, Verifi) to stop cases becoming chargebacks at all, and access to the networks of record (VROL, MasterCom) — plus a staffed team working to hard scheme deadlines.

08–10Credit, data, and staying alive

Credit: bureau data from Experian, Equifax and TransUnion; open banking from TrueLayer (UK/EU data and payments, VRP), Tink (Visa-owned, 3,400+ institutions across 18 markets), Yapily (white-label, 19 countries) or GoCardless Bank Account Data; decisioning on Taktile, Provenir, Oscilar, GoRules or OPA. Keep model and policy separately versioned, and share the feature store with fincrime so "average monthly inflow" is computed once.

Data: Kafka or Redpanda, Flink, dbt, and BigQuery, Snowflake or Databricks. Feature platform from Chronon (Apache 2.0, and it powers 100% of Stripe's charge-path fraud models), Feast, Tecton or Hopsworks. The rule: the decision path must never depend on the analytics path — the warehouse can be down without declining payments.

The same ten layers, three real banks

Sophistication does not predict build-versus-buy. Read across any row and the assumption falls apart.

Three deliberately different shapes: Monzo holds a licence and builds nearly everything. Revolut holds a licence and built its own ledger but buys card processing. Chime holds no banking licence at all — and built its own processor anyway.

A gap worth naming

No neobank in this research publicly documents its dispute platform. Fraud stacks are written about extensively; the thing that handles chargebacks — the layer carrying hard regulatory deadlines and real balance-sheet exposure — is never named.

That silence is itself informative. Disputes are treated as back-office operations rather than as engineering, which is exactly why the funnel above is where the advantage still sits.

What the comparison actually teaches

  • Licence and technology are orthogonal. Chime is the proof: no charter, but it owns the fee-bearing layer sitting directly above the regulated boundary.
  • Everyone builds the ledger. All three did — the only universal "build" in the table, and the layer with the least vendor substitution among serious operators.
  • Everyone buys identity. Monzo and Revolut both use Onfido. Nobody builds document verification; it is commodity data with a forensics problem attached.
  • Fincrime splits on liability. The two UK banks built their own detection — because APP reimbursement makes fraud a direct P&L line. Chime, with no such regime, buys from Unit21.
  • Only one built continuity. Monzo's second-cloud Stand-in has no public counterpart anywhere in this research.

Where do chargebacks sit?

Not on one layer. A chargeback is a plane that cuts through five of them — which is exactly why it gets under-designed.

Ask most teams where disputes live and they point at financial crime. That is only where the case lives. The liability is set three layers lower, the money moves through the ledger twice, and the recovery has to reconcile at the bottom. Every one of those is a different team.

The layer-by-layer answer

LayerWhat chargebacks demand of it
02 — Scheme accessSets the liability. As your own principal member you are the issuer of record and the loss is yours, with no sponsor to absorb or contest it. With a BIN sponsor the sponsor is liable to the scheme, then recovers from you by contract with allocation by root cause
03 — Core ledgerTwo postings, not one. A provisional credit at intake — regulatory timelines force it, so the customer has the money back while the case is open — then a reversal or confirmation at resolution. Your ledger must model a contingent balance, and reversal must be clean months later
04 — Card processingDelivers the message. The processor receives the chargeback from the scheme and exposes the workflow. Thredd, Marqeta and Galileo all do this by API — enough for a first programme, rarely enough at volume
07 — DisputesWhere the case lives. Evidence, representment, pre-arbitration, and the hard scheme deadlines. Platforms: Quavo, Pega Smart Dispute, Rivero. Pre-dispute: Ethoca and Verifi, which stop cases becoming chargebacks at all
10 — ReconciliationCloses the loop. A won chargeback is money arriving that must match a scheme settlement file. Dispute recoveries are their own break class — and one of the easiest to leave unreconciled for months

The design consequence

Because a chargeback crosses five layers and four teams, nobody owns it end to end by default — and the failure shows up not as a lost case but as an unexplained break, months later, in a reconciliation nobody connected to disputes.

Name an owner for the whole plane, not for the case-management tool. And model the provisional credit as a contingent position on day one: it is real money, on your book, on a case you have not yet won.

Building best-in-class dispute management

The industry resolves 26% of disputes before they become chargebacks. Best-in-class inverts that number — and almost none of the work happens in the case-management tool.

Two statistics reframe the whole problem. 75% of customers go straight to their bank rather than the merchant — so you are the front door. And 73.6% of disputes become chargebacks; only 26.4% are resolved before one is filed. Every case you deflect at the top costs a fraction of one you fight at the bottom.

So build it as a funnel, and spend your effort at the widest part.

The clock you must never miss — and it is not the scheme's

There are two independent deadline systems, and the regulatory one binds first and harder. This is the single most under-appreciated fact in dispute design.

United Kingdom — PSRs 2017United States — Regulation E
Refund the customerBy the end of the business day following the day you become aware of an unauthorised transactionProvisional credit within 10 business days of notification (20 for new accounts)
Investigation windowInvestigate after refunding — the refund is not contingent on the outcome10 business days; extendable to 45 days if provisional credit is given — 90 days for debit POS and foreign ATM
Withholding itOnly if the payer acted fraudulently, or with intent or gross negligence — and you must provide supporting evidenceRecoverable if the investigation finds no error, with notice
Notification dutiesRefund charges and lost interest tooNotify within 2 business days of provisional credit; correct within 1 of finding an error; report within 3 of completing

The design consequence for a UK bank

You refund first and investigate afterwards. Next business day, unless you can evidence fraud or gross negligence — and the burden of that evidence is yours.

That makes two things architectural rather than operational: a deadline engine that cannot fail silently, and a ledger that models the refund as a contingent position from the moment it posts. A UK issuer carries dispute exposure on its book far earlier than a US one, and for cases it may never win.

Where first-party fraud changed the game

The uncomfortable trend: first-party misuse has risen to roughly 36% of reported fraud, from about 15% in 2023, and some estimates put the share of disputes far higher. Meanwhile 83% of issuers say rising customer awareness of the dispute process is itself driving volume. Customers have learned that disputing works.

Two consequences you must design for:

  • Detecting your own customers. Repeat disputers, dispute-after-delivery patterns, and disputes clustering on a device or address. This is a model, not a rule — and it is uncomfortable, because the subject is a customer you are also trying to retain.
  • Merchants now fight back with evidence. Compelling Evidence 3.0 lets a merchant link a disputed transaction to prior legitimate purchases from the same device and account. Full CE 3.0 evidence overturns 40–60% of eligible disputes, against 15–20% for a receipt alone. If your case files cannot answer that, you will lose cases you used to win.

The flywheel most issuers never build

Dispute outcomes are labelled training data. A confirmed fraud chargeback is ground truth — the cleanest label your fraud models will ever get, arriving free, continuously, and correctly timed. Stripe's structural advantage on the acquiring side is exactly this: labels arrive automatically when cardholders dispute.

Wire disputes back into three places and the system compounds: fraud model training, merchant risk scoring built from your own outcomes rather than a bought list, and first-party fraud detection. Most issuers treat disputes as a cost centre and let this data die in a case-management tool.

The metrics that tell you if it is working

Deflection rate — resolved before a chargeback exists, against the 26.4% industry baseline. This is the headline number. Cost per case, fully loaded with analyst time. Win rate by reason code, not in aggregate — an aggregate win rate hides which reason codes you are systematically losing. Time to provisional credit as a compliance metric, not an efficiency one. First-party share and repeat-disputer rate. And aged unreconciled recoveries, which is where the money quietly goes missing.

Buy the case platform. Build the funnel around it — that is where best-in-class actually lives, and none of it is a feature on a vendor comparison sheet.

The decisions, ranked by regret cost

  1. The ledger's invariants and balance checkpointing. Retrofitting is an online migration of your most sensitive table.
  2. Reconciliation as a first-class subsystem. Both the FCA and the FDIC independently mandate it, and no vendor encodes your break taxonomy.
  3. Card scheme principal membership timing. Millions a year in dues, and a long lead time you cannot compress.
  4. Prefunding and treasury. A continuously managed position, not a deposit — and materially cheaper since July 2026.
  5. Whether fincrime detection is yours. In the UK, APP liability decides this for you.
  6. Cloud continuity. Cheap if scoped ruthlessly, impossible to retrofit credibly.

What independence actually buys

No sponsor can exit your programme, cap your balances at payroll peaks, or be ordered out of the business by its regulator. You settle in your own name. You are the issuer of record.

In exchange you carry capital requirements, scheme dues, treasury operations, 24/7 money on-call, dispute liability with nobody to share it, and a regulatory reporting obligation that is itself a product.

Independence is a cost structure, not an architecture. The architecture question underneath is unchanged, and it is the same one every configuration faces: which dependency can you least afford?
