Neobank stack · deep dive

Monzo payments, end to end

The most completely documented payments stack in retail banking — and a six-year insourcing arc where every move was triggered by a constraint, never by cost.

18 Aug 20263 rails insourced14 sources

Monzo has published more about how its payments actually work than any other bank. Read it in sequence and a single pattern emerges: it took every rail in-house, one at a time, over six years — cards in 2017–18, Faster Payments in 2019–20, Bacs in 2022. Today it settles against its own Bank of England account and has no UK sponsor at all.

What makes the sequence instructive is why each move happened.

01The payment that isn't a payment

Between two Monzo customers, no rail is involved at all. That single fact explains most of the product.

02The gateway is a monolith, on purpose

At a company running more than three thousand microservices, the Faster Payments gateway is deliberately none of them.

PropertyChoice
ShapeA monolithic replicated application in Go — explicitly not microservices
TopologyActive-active across two data centres (was active-standby, ~15 min failover)
ConnectivityPer-payment-type TCP to the FPS hub; two physical links per data centre, tolerating 3 of 4 failing
DeploymentDocker and Docker Compose on virtual machines — not Kubernetes
StorageDRBD — three servers per DC, dual SSDs, six replicas per write
SecurityFour HSMs, two per DC; the gateway still runs on one
ResilienceStore-and-forward stand-in — answers the hub on a per-message timeout, forwards when the processor recovers
Observability10-second metrics, 60-second alerting, logs to BigQuery, dedicated payments on-call

The migration completed in one hour against a three-hour window.

Why a monolith. A scheme gateway is a different problem from a product estate: a small fixed protocol surface, an extreme availability requirement, and hard dependencies on physical connectivity and HSMs. Distributing it adds failure modes without adding capability. Monzo runs >3,000 microservices and this — the lesson being that architectural style should follow the problem, not the house style.

The detail worth stealing

Store-and-forward at the gateway. If the internal payment processor is slow, the gateway answers the scheme hub itself on a timeout, then forwards the message once things recover.

Without it, an internal wobble becomes a rejected customer payment and a scheme-level performance problem. With it, internal slowness stays internal.

03Bacs, and the cap that forced it

Monzo used a sponsor bank from 2017. Two problems ended that: manual money transfers to the sponsor carried operational risk, and regulatory limits capped how much Monzo could hold at the sponsor during mid-month and end-of-month payroll peaks.

Eighteen months of work across engineering, operations, support, risk, legal and infrastructure produced a SWIFT Banking Network connection to Bacs, HSMs holding the certificates that sign and verify files, Bacs microservices that generate files and hash them with the HSM signing the hash before transmission, and direct settlement against Monzo's own Bank of England account — removing the sponsor from the money path entirely. Migration day was 12 September 2022, chosen for low volume; the first Direct Debit file landed at 00:19 the next morning.

04International — adapters, deciders, effects

adapter  →  deciders  →  effects  →  effect application
   │           │            │              │
 partner    Accept /     ledger entries,  executed
 formats    Reject /     notifications,   and logged
 normalised Hold        movements       for audit

Deciders run in parallel and return Accept, Reject or Hold with encoded precedence — Hold blocks, Reject overrides Accept — rather than depending on evaluation order. Effects are generated from the decision and applied separately, which is what makes the pipeline testable as a closed box.

40+ currencies without holding an account in each: a correspondent partner bank receives the payment, performs the exchange, and forwards over SWIFT with the rate attached. IBAN checksums, BIC codes — Monzo's is MONZGB2L — and idempotency for exactly-once. Each partner gets an adapter plus a separate microservice for its quirks, so onboarding one never touches the core processor.

05Two very different spikes

Crowdfunding, 2019. Built for 100,000 investments in five minutes and 1,000 per second at peak — 15–30× normal load. The architecture put an in-memory balance-cache LRU in front of approximate balance checks specifically to keep Cassandra out of the pre-investment path entirely, with NSQ absorbing the burst and a rate-limited consumer writing into the same ledger service the bank uses. Actual result: £4m in under two minutes.

Payday. "Get Paid Early" lets customers pull Bacs credits at 4pm the day before they are due, producing up to a 500% traffic increase in seconds across up to 250,000 eligible payments — then a cascade as those customers immediately pay bills and move money into pots. Reactive autoscaling cannot respond fast enough, so Monzo forecasts the day's demand from the incoming Bacs records themselves and uses Jaeger traces to find every downstream service that needs headroom.

The pairing is the lesson. One spike is unpredictable, and is absorbed by taking the database out of the hot path. The other is scheduled by an external rail — so it is absorbed by reading the future off the rail and pre-scaling into it.

06The constraint underneath everything

Monzo's ledger addresses are a five-tuple, and a balance means summing every entry in the group. By 2022 that was P99 400–500ms — and during card authorisation, a slow balance read does not fail safe. The scheme invokes its own stand-in and approves on the issuer's behalf, potentially beyond available balance, with the issuer's fraud checks never running.

Fixed with pre-computed balance blocks and time-series reindexing. P99 fell to ~200ms. In a closed-loop bank where most transfers never touch a rail, the ledger read path is the payment performance story.

What to steal

  1. Insource on constraints, not on cost. Every Monzo move was triggered by a specific operational limit — a vendor's reliability, a failover time, a balance cap.
  2. Let the rail gateway be a monolith. Different problem, different architecture.
  3. Store-and-forward at the gateway, so internal slowness never becomes a scheme-level failure.
  4. Precedence as data in decisioning — Hold blocks, Reject overrides Accept.
  5. Generate effects separately from applying them.
  6. Keep the database out of the spike path — and when an external rail tells you what is coming, pre-scale from it.
  7. Server-side eligibility, so payment changes ship without an app release.

Still unknown

Whether Monzo is a CHAPS direct participant — FPS and Bacs are confirmed, CHAPS is not established either way. Whether it holds Mastercard principal membership in the UK, and what its in-house processor actually covers: authorisation only, or clearing and settlement too. Who the Bacs sponsor was between 2017 and 2022, and who provided the pre-2019 Faster Payments gateway — Monzo has never named either. And whether Stand-in handles international payments or domestic rails only.
