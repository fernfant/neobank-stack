Who runs what

The third-party vendors across every layer of a neobank stack — and, underneath each one, which banks are actually named as using them.

18 Aug 202643 vendors8 layers16 sources

The vendor landscape is well documented. Which neobank uses which vendor is not — and that asymmetry is the point of this page. Vendors publish logos; banks rarely confirm. Everything below traces to a company statement, a vendor's own named customer list, a filing, or credible trade press, with the confidence marked. Where nothing is public, it says so instead of guessing.

In the map, a solid box means at least one bank is publicly named. A faded, dashed box means no neobank attribution could be found — which usually means customers who contractually avoid being named, not a vendor without customers.

The one row with a strategic story

Galileo powers Chime, Robinhood and Varo — and it is owned by SoFi, which competes with all three. Chime's decision to insource its processor as ChimeCore is exactly what that conflict predicts, and Marqeta is watching the same dynamic play out from the other side as Block winds its programme down. Ask any issuer-processor whether its other customers are your competitors; the answer is a real input, not a debating point.

A necessary caveat on "built in-house"

The FCA fined Monzo £21m in 2025 for failing to design, implement and maintain adequate customer onboarding, customer risk assessment and transaction monitoring systems. The most sophisticated public fincrime architecture in this entire research belongs to a bank that was penalised for control failings.

Both things are true at once, and a vendor map showing only the first would be misleading. Building your own platform is not the same as building an adequate one — and buying a vendor does not discharge the obligation either.

Four limits on any map like this

  1. Absence of a name is not absence of a customer. Most banks contractually avoid being named, especially in financial crime and identity.
  2. Vendor logo walls go stale, and nobody announces a departure. Chime still appears on Galileo material while moving to ChimeCore; Marqeta's Block programme is winding down. Assume every logo is at least a year behind.
  3. "Uses vendor X" hides the scope. Galileo powering "the US business of Monzo" is a completely different claim from Galileo powering Monzo. Scope qualifiers matter far more than logos.
  4. Nobody publishes what they pay. Every cost question in this research remains open.

What would improve this map

Five specific unknowns, each answerable by one good source: which fincrime vendor Monzo runs alongside its in-house platform after the fine; who processes Chime's debit volume in 2026 (Galileo, ChimeCore, or split); whether Revolut uses Thredd at home or only for specific programmes; which identity vendors Chime, Starling and Zopa use; and what replaced Cash App's Marqeta programme.
